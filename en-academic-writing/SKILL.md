# Writing Natural English Academic Text

Goal: produce text that reads as genuinely human-written, free of AI tell-tale signs.

---

## I. FILE FORMATTING (Word / LaTeX)

### Font and color
- Font: **Times New Roman**, 12pt, pure black only
- NO colored text, NO colored table headers, NO cell shading
- Line spacing: 1.5 or double (as instructed), left margin 2.54cm, right margin 2.54cm
- First-line indent: 1.27cm, no extra blank lines between paragraphs

### Characters NEVER to use
| Forbidden | Replace with |
|---|---|
| Em dash — or en dash – | Comma, semicolon, or rewrite the sentence |
| \*\*word\*\* (Markdown bold) | Actual bold formatting tag in docx/LaTeX |
| Tilde ~ and pipe \| outside tables | Rewrite the sentence |
| Bullet points – or • | Numbered list or inline prose enumeration |
| Markdown syntax ##, \`\`\`, \*\* | Remove entirely from output |

### Tables
- Simple borders only, no fill color, no colored header text
- Header row: bold text, white background, black border

---

## II. LANGUAGE AND STYLE

### Sentence structure
- Vary sentence length deliberately: mix short sentences (5–10 words), medium (15–20 words), and occasional long complex ones with subordinate clauses
- Never repeat the same sentence structure in two consecutive sentences
- Avoid the default AI pattern: [Topic phrase] + [is/are] + [noun phrase] repeated every sentence

### Words and phrases to AVOID (AI-typical vocabulary)
- "It is worth noting that...", "It is important to note that..."
- "In today's rapidly evolving world...", "In the context of..."
- "Structured", "systematically", "genuinely", "evidenced-based" used as padding
- "Moreover", "Furthermore", "Additionally" stacked in the same paragraph
- "In conclusion, it is clear that..." as an opening to the conclusion
- Overly complex noun phrases that exceed the register of the audience: "the multidimensional optimization of stakeholder-centric learning outcomes"
- "Plays a crucial role", "of paramount importance", "cannot be overstated"

### Voice and person
- Use a consistent point of view throughout: "we", "the authors", "this paper" — do not mix them
- Avoid excessive passive voice: "was conducted", "was examined", "was applied" in consecutive sentences — alternate with active constructions
- Sentences can occasionally be short and direct — not every sentence needs to be "smooth and polished"

### Paragraph transitions
- Use natural connectors: "however", "as a result", "in contrast", "specifically", "this suggests"
- Do NOT open every paragraph with a connector — topic sentences can stand alone
- Do not use the same connector more than twice in one section

---

## III. STRUCTURE

- Do not open any section with a dictionary definition ("According to Merriam-Webster, X is defined as...")
- Do not conclude by restating every point already made — synthesize or reflect instead
- Avoid repeating the same three-part structure for every subsection: [Definition] → [List of features] → [Example]
- Body paragraphs must flow from one to the next, not simply be stacked list items

---

## IV. PRE-OUTPUT CHECKLIST

Before delivering the final result, self-check each item:

- [ ] Does any sentence sound like an AI template? Rewrite it.
- [ ] Are there any em dashes (— or –)? Remove or replace.
- [ ] Is any connector word used more than twice in one paragraph? Cut it.
- [ ] Is the point of view (we / the authors / this paper) consistent throughout?
- [ ] Are there more than two consecutive passive-voice sentences? Rewrite in active voice.
- [ ] Are there any Markdown symbols (\*\*, ##, ~, \`\`\`) in the output? Remove all.
- [ ] Does the conclusion do anything beyond restating the body? If not, revise.
